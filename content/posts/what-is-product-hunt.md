Launching a new product can be challenging, especially for startups without an established audience. Even a well-designed application may struggle to attract users if few people know it exists.

Product Hunt offers a way to introduce new products to people interested in discovering technology. Companies including Loom and Notion have used the platform to attract users, gather feedback, and build communities around their products.

## 1. What Is Product Hunt?

[Product Hunt](https://www.producthunt.com/) is an online platform where founders, developers, and other creators introduce technology products to potential users.

Launched in 2013, it has become a popular destination for discovering software, mobile applications, productivity tools, artificial intelligence products, and other technology.

Each day, creators submit products that visitors can explore, upvote, and discuss.

A typical listing includes the product’s name, a short description, screenshots or demonstrations, and a link to its website. Visitors can ask questions, share feedback, and interact with the people who built it.

Product Hunt also recognizes popular launches through daily rankings and distinctions such as *Product of the Day*.

However, submitting a product does not guarantee placement on the featured homepage leaderboard. Product Hunt’s team selects which launches to feature, and rankings consider engagement signals rather than simply counting raw upvotes.

Submitting a product is free.

## 2. Why Do Startups Launch on Product Hunt?

Product Hunt gives founders access to an audience interested in exploring new technology.

For an early-stage company, this can create several opportunities.

**Finding early users.** A launch may introduce a product to people willing to experiment with new software before it has widespread recognition.

**Gathering feedback.** Visitors can identify useful features, ask questions, and highlight problems the founders may have overlooked.

**Generating visibility.** A launch can produce website traffic, newsletter subscriptions, trial accounts, or paying customers.

**Building relationships.** Founders may connect with entrepreneurs, developers, potential collaborators, and investors.

Product Hunt can be especially useful for products whose intended users participate in technology communities.

However, attention on the platform does not necessarily translate into lasting commercial success. A product may receive hundreds of upvotes without generating substantial revenue or retaining its initial users.

For that reason, founders may find customer engagement and retention more informative than leaderboard performance alone.

## 3. How to Prepare for a Product Hunt Launch

Launching on Product Hunt involves more than submitting a website link. Founders typically prepare their product, presentation materials, and outreach before launch day.

### Create a Product Hunt Account

Founders can submit their own products using personal Product Hunt accounts. Recruiting someone else to post the product is not necessary.

Participating in the community before launching can also help founders understand how other creators introduce their products and engage with prospective users.

### Prepare Your Product Page

A Product Hunt listing generally includes:

- **Product name:** The name users will recognize.
- **Tagline:** A concise explanation of what the product does.
- **Description:** Additional information about the product and its intended audience.
- **Screenshots or demonstrations:** Visual materials showing how the product works.
- **Product link:** A destination where visitors can explore or access the offering.
- **Maker comment:** An introduction explaining why the product was created and what feedback would be helpful.

A clear explanation of the problem the product solves is often more useful than an extensive technical description.

### Make Sure the Product Is Ready

Product Hunt generally favors products that visitors can meaningfully experience. A product does not necessarily need to be feature-complete, but it should offer something sufficiently developed for the community to evaluate.

For example, a founder launching a SaaS application might verify that visitors can create accounts, access its advertised features, and use its basic functionality.

An attractive listing may generate interest, but that attention has limited value if visitors cannot successfully experience the product.

### Choose a Launch Date

Product Hunt allows founders to schedule launches in advance. Under its current procedures, scheduled launches go live at 12:01 a.m. Pacific Time on the selected date.

Founders often choose a date when they can devote sufficient time to answering questions and interacting with visitors.

Establishing goals beforehand can also help. These might include attracting early users, gathering feedback, or generating product demonstrations.

## 4. What Happens on Launch Day?

When a product goes live, visitors can explore its listing, visit its website, and participate in discussions.

Founders commonly introduce themselves through a maker comment explaining why they built the product and what they hope to learn from the community.

They may also share the launch with existing customers, newsletter subscribers, social media followers, and other interested audiences.

However, Product Hunt distinguishes legitimate promotion from attempts to manipulate rankings.

The platform prohibits directly soliciting upvotes, paying for engagement, or rewarding users for voting. Founders can instead invite people to explore their products and provide genuine feedback.

Throughout the day, creators can respond to questions, clarify features, and engage with prospective users.

For many startups, these interactions may provide more lasting value than the number of upvotes received.

## 5. Successful Companies That Launched on Product Hunt

Several well-known technology companies have used Product Hunt during their early development or to introduce significant product releases.

### Loom: From Openvid to a Video Communication Platform

Loom originally launched on Product Hunt in 2016 under the name Openvid.

According to Product Hunt, approximately 3,000 people signed up within the first 24 hours.

The founders subsequently used feedback and continued engagement with the community to refine their product and better understand its users.

Loom eventually became a widely used workplace video communication platform and was acquired by Atlassian in 2023.

Its experience illustrates how Product Hunt can help a young company reach early adopters and learn from their responses.

### Notion: Building Momentum Through Product Launches

Notion, the collaborative productivity platform, also has a long history with Product Hunt.

The company introduced early versions of its workspace software through the platform and launched Notion 2.0 in 2018.

That release became one of Product Hunt’s most popular launches, earning Product of the Day, Week, and Month recognition.

Notion’s team has credited Product Hunt’s community with providing valuable feedback and helping introduce the product to new audiences.

Its experience also illustrates that Product Hunt is not limited to a company’s first public debut. Founders may return to the platform to introduce major updates or new products.

Neither company’s eventual success can be attributed solely to Product Hunt. Product development, customer retention, and broader business execution remained essential.

## 6. What Happens After the Launch?

A Product Hunt launch may produce a temporary increase in attention. The more important question is whether that interest leads to meaningful results.

Several measures can help founders evaluate a launch:

- **Website traffic:** How many visitors arrived through Product Hunt?
- **Signups:** How many visitors created accounts or joined a mailing list?
- **Activation:** How many users experienced the product’s core features?
- **Conversions:** How many visitors became paying customers, where applicable?
- **Retention:** How many users continued using the product after launch?
- **Feedback:** What did visitors identify as useful, confusing, or missing?

These measures tell different stories.

A launch generating 1,000 signups but little continued usage may indicate that the product attracted attention without retaining users. A smaller launch that produces several enthusiastic customers and detailed feedback may be more valuable to a company still refining its offering.

Customer responses can also influence future development. Founders may discover that users value an unexpected feature or that the product’s positioning does not clearly communicate its purpose.

Product Hunt can therefore serve as both a discovery channel and an opportunity to learn from prospective users.

## Conclusion

Product Hunt gives startups an opportunity to introduce new products, reach early adopters, and gather feedback from a community interested in emerging technology.

Companies like Loom and Notion demonstrate how the platform can contribute to visibility and product development. However, a successful launch is only one part of building a sustainable business.

For founders, Product Hunt’s greatest value may come from the users it introduces to their products and the feedback that helps guide what they build next.
