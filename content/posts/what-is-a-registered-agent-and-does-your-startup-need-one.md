When forming a limited liability company (LLC) or corporation, founders typically must designate a registered agent. The requirement is straightforward, and appointing one is usually inexpensive.

## What Is a Registered Agent?

A registered agent is a person or business designated to receive legal documents and certain official government correspondence on behalf of a company.

For example, if someone files a lawsuit against your startup, the registered agent may receive the summons and complaint through a process known as *service of process*.

Registered agents may also receive official notices concerning the company's legal status or state filing obligations.

States generally require LLCs and corporations to maintain a registered agent with a physical address in the relevant state who is available during ordinary business hours.

A registered agent is not necessarily responsible for managing your company, providing legal advice, or handling all its business correspondence.

## Can You Be Your Own Registered Agent?

In many states, yes.

Founders can often serve as their companies' registered agents if they satisfy applicable state requirements, commonly including maintaining an eligible physical address in the state and being available during business hours.

Serving as your own registered agent eliminates the cost of hiring a commercial service, but there are practical disadvantages.

First, the registered agent's address typically appears in public state records. Founders working from home may prefer not to disclose their residential addresses. Hiring a commercial agent can help protect that information, although a founder's address may still appear in other public filings.

Second, the registered agent must be reliably available to receive important legal documents. Founders who travel frequently or lack a consistent business location may find this inconvenient.

For these reasons, many startups use professional registered agent services.

## What If Your Startup Is Incorporated in Another State?

Your company generally must maintain a registered agent in its state of formation, even if it operates elsewhere.

For example, suppose you incorporate your startup in Delaware but operate the business from Tennessee.

You will need a registered agent with an appropriate physical address in Delaware.

If your company must also register to do business in Tennessee, it will generally need a registered agent there as well.

This is one reason startups incorporated in Delaware frequently use professional registered agent services, particularly when their founders live in other states.

## What Happens If You Don't Maintain a Registered Agent?

Failing to maintain a registered agent can create legal and administrative problems.

Depending on the state, consequences may include penalties, loss of good standing, or administrative dissolution. A company that fails to receive important legal notices may also miss deadlines for responding to litigation.

Hiring a registered agent does not, by itself, ensure compliance with state filing or tax requirements. Founders remain responsible for understanding and satisfying those obligations.

## Choosing a Registered Agent

Serving as your own registered agent may be reasonable if you maintain an eligible in-state address and can reliably receive legal documents. Otherwise, a professional service offers a relatively inexpensive alternative.

The most important consideration is reliability. Your registered agent should provide a dependable way for legal notices and official correspondence to reach your company promptly.

Maintaining accurate registered agent information is a simple but important part of keeping your business in good standing.
